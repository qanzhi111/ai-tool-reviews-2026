Web3 Security Investigation: How to Trace Stolen Crypto and Detect Rug Pulls

The decentralized finance ecosystem lost over $1.8 billion to hacks and scams in 2025 alone. From sophisticated smart contract exploits to social engineering campaigns, the threats keep evolving — and most victims never recover their funds.

But here's what most people don't realize: blockchain transactions are public. Every transfer, every wallet, every contract interaction is permanently recorded and freely accessible. The same transparency that makes crypto appealing also makes it investigable.

Whether you've been affected by a scam or you want to understand how on-chain forensics actually works, this article walks you through the core methodology.

How On-Chain Investigation Works: The INVEST Framework

Professional investigators follow a structured methodology. Here's a simplified version:

1. Identify the Alert

The trigger could be anything: a sudden TVL drop, a community report, or an unusual transaction. The key is speed — the faster you start tracing, the more likely funds haven't been laundered through mixers yet.

2. Navigate the Transaction Flow

Using block explorers like Etherscan, trace the flow of funds from the victim's wallet or exploited contract. Look for:

  • Immediate transfers to the attacker's wallet
  • Conversion to privacy coins or mixing services
  • Distribution across multiple wallets (peeling chains)

3. Verify the Exploit

If a smart contract was exploited, read the contract code. Look for:

  • Reentrancy vulnerabilities
  • Access control flaws
  • Flash loan attack patterns
  • Unusual function calls in the attack transaction

4. Establish Connections

Link wallets and addresses using:

  • Funding source analysis (which wallets funded the attacker?)
  • Timing correlations
  • Shared withdrawal patterns to centralized exchanges

5. Secure the Evidence

Document everything:

  • Screenshot all relevant transactions
  • Record wallet addresses and timestamps
  • Save contract code at the time of exploit
  • Note any off-chain communications

6. Take Action

Depending on your findings:

  • Report to relevant exchanges for wallet freezing
  • File with law enforcement if applicable
  • Submit to bug bounty platforms
  • Contact the project team for responsible disclosure

A Real-World Example: How a Rug Pull Investigation Unfolds

Let's say a project's token price suddenly drops 95%. Here's what an investigator does:

  1. Check liquidity pool — The LP tokens were removed, draining the trading pair
  2. Trace the removed funds — The developer wallet moved LP tokens to a fresh address
  3. Follow the conversion — The stolen tokens were swapped for ETH through a DEX aggregator
  4. Identify the exit — ETH was sent to a centralized exchange (CEX) for cash-out
  5. Document and report — The CEX is notified with transaction evidence, potentially freezing the account

The entire investigation might take 2-4 hours. Speed matters because once funds hit a mixer like Tornado Cash, tracing becomes significantly harder.
